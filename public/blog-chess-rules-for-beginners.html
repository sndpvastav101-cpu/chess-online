Chess Rules for Beginners

A plain-language guide to get you from zero to your first game.

Chess is played on an 8x8 board between two players, White and Black. White always moves first, and players alternate turns from there. The goal is to checkmate your opponent's king - putting it under attack in a way it cannot escape.

How each piece moves

  • Pawn: moves forward one square (two on its first move), and captures diagonally one square forward. Pawns cannot move backward.
  • Rook: moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically.
  • Bishop: moves any number of squares diagonally, and stays on one color for the entire game.
  • Knight: moves in an "L" shape - two squares in one direction, then one square perpendicular. It's the only piece that can jump over others.
  • Queen: combines the rook and bishop - any number of squares in any straight or diagonal direction. It's the most powerful piece on the board.
  • King: moves one square in any direction. Keeping it safe is the entire point of the game.

How a game ends

A game can end in a few ways:

  • Checkmate: a king is under attack (in "check") and has no legal move to escape it. This ends the game immediately - the side delivering checkmate wins.
  • Resignation: a player can resign at any point if they believe their position is lost.
  • Draw: games can end in a draw by agreement, stalemate (a player has no legal move but isn't in check), repetition of the same position three times, or insufficient material to checkmate.
  • Time out: in timed games, running out of the clock counts as a loss (unless your opponent also lacks enough material to checkmate).

Special moves that confuse new players

Castling lets the king and a rook move at the same time - the king moves two squares toward the rook, and the rook jumps to the other side of it. It's allowed once per game, only if neither piece has moved yet, the squares between them are empty, and the king isn't in check or passing through check.

En passant is a special pawn capture. If an opponent's pawn moves two squares forward and lands beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had only moved one square - but only on the very next move.

Promotion happens when a pawn reaches the far end of the board. It's immediately upgraded to a queen, rook, bishop, or knight of the same color - almost always a queen, since it's the strongest piece.
