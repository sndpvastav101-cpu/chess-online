7 Practical Tips to Improve at Chess

None of these require a coach or a book - just a change in habits.

1. Slow down before you move

The single biggest source of avoidable losses at a casual level isn't a lack of knowledge - it's moving too fast. Before every move, take a few seconds to check: what does this move allow my opponent to do? A one-move blunder check catches the majority of hanging pieces and missed threats.

2. Look at every check, capture, and threat

When you're scanning a position, specifically look for checks, captures, and threats - both yours and your opponent's. These "forcing moves" change the position the most and are where tactics usually hide, even if the move you end up playing is something quieter.

3. Develop your pieces before attacking

A common beginner pattern is launching an early attack with just a queen or a single piece. It rarely works against a player who defends calmly, and it often leaves your own position underdeveloped. Get your knights and bishops out, castle your king to safety, then look for a plan.

4. Play longer time controls while you're learning

Bullet and blitz are fun, but they punish thinking and reward pattern memorization you may not have yet. While you're still learning core ideas, rapid or classical time controls give you room to actually apply what you know instead of just reacting on instinct.

5. Review your losses, not just your wins

It's tempting to move on quickly after a loss, but that's exactly where the useful lessons are. After a game, look back at the moment things went wrong - was it one blunder, or a slow build-up of small inaccuracies? You don't need deep engine analysis to benefit from this; just asking "what happened here?" is often enough.

6. Learn a few endgames on purpose

Basic endgames - like king and pawn vs. king, or how to checkmate with a rook - come up constantly and are simple to learn once and remember forever. Knowing them removes a lot of guesswork in the final phase of a game, where many close games are actually decided.

7. Play consistently, not occasionally

Improvement in chess tracks with repetition more than any single trick. A few focused games a week, with a little reflection afterward, will move your rating more reliably than an occasional long session with no follow-up.

Put it into practice

The fastest way to build these habits is simply to play more thoughtful games. Start a match on ChessOnline and try applying just one of these tips at a time.
